Verdigris Bay

A procedurally generated cyberpunk survival RPG that runs in your terminal. Everything below is how the city actually works: the dice, the chrome, the wire, and the six factions who will remember what you did. Read what you need, then go down to the water.

00Getting Started

You need Python 3.10 or newer. Nothing else. No pip install, no virtual environment, no compiled extensions.

python3 play.py                      # opens the character browser
python3 play.py mysave.db            # load/create a specific save file
python3 play.py mysave.db --seed 42  # reproducible world generation

Run with no arguments and you get a character browser: every saved run, listed with its name, handle, role, level, district, turn, difficulty and whether it's still alive. Pick one to load it, or start a new character. New characters are saved under a saves/ folder, one file each, named after the character. The game autosaves as you play; you never have to remember to.

Leaving the game Type menu at the prompt — or hit Ctrl+C anywhere, even mid-fight — to open the menu: Resume, Change theme, Save now, or Save and quit. Your world is always saved; combat is the only thing you'd abandon by bailing out of a fight.
Accessibility & terminals Colour and Unicode are auto-detected and degrade cleanly. If glyphs or colours render badly, set an environment variable before launching:
  • NO_COLOR=1 — turn off all colour (monochrome text).
  • VERDIGRIS_ASCII=1 — use the plain ASCII glyph set instead of Unicode.
  • VERDIGRIS_FORCE_COLOR=1 — force colour even when output is piped.
Colour is disabled automatically when output is not a terminal or when TERM=dumb. You can also pick the Mono theme in-game for the same effect.

02The Turn Loop

Verdigris Bay is turn-based, but time only moves when you do something meaningful. Looking at your sheet, checking inventory, or reading the journal are free. The actions that advance a world turn are: explore travel net market clinic story use wait.

On each world turn, in this exact order:

  1. Status effects tick — bleeding and burns deal damage, timed effects count down, a stim wears off into its crash. You can bleed out between fights.
  2. Trace decays — and if your trace is high enough, consequences may fire (up to a strike team at your door).
  3. Heat decays — slowly where you're standing, faster in districts you've left.
  4. Story triggers re-evaluate — the narrative engine checks whether world state has opened a new thread.
  5. Random encounter roll — weighted by district, heat, and your reputation.
Why the order matters Statuses resolve before encounters, so a wound can drop you before the next fight begins. Story beats are checked before random content, so the plot takes precedence over noise.

03Checks & Dice

Every uncertain action resolves the same way. One formula runs the whole game.

total = d10 + attribute + skill + situational modifiers
success if total ≥ DV (Difficulty Value)

Swing dice

The d10 is not flat. A natural 10 explodes: roll again and add, and keep going while you roll 10s. A natural 1 implodes: roll again and subtract. This is the street luck the city runs on — a desperate amateur can occasionally beat a wall, and a professional can occasionally faceplant.

Margin, criticals and fumbles

  • Margin is how far you cleared (or missed) the DV. Degree of success matters everywhere: it adds damage, yields more intel, and moves netrun outcomes.
  • Critical — the die swung upward and you beat the DV by 5 or more. Exceptional result.
  • Fumble — the die swung downward and you missed by 5 or more. It goes badly.

Difficulty Values

DV bands
BandDVFeel
trivial7You'd have to be actively unlucky to miss.

routine12The baseline. A competent hand clears it most of the time.
tricky15Takes some training or talent.

nightmare26Needs heavy investment or a lucky swing.

Calibration: an unspecialised but capable character (attribute 5, skill 3) averages 13.5 on the roll, clearing routine roughly 70% of the time. A specialist (attribute 8, skill 4) clears it almost always.

04Attributes & Skills

Seven attributes, range 1–10. Every skill is governed by one of them; a check adds the attribute and the skill rank. Cyberware, status effects and your Coherence band can raise or lower an effective attribute, so the number you roll with is not always your base.

Skills run 0–10 and are raised with skill points earned at level-up (see §12). You can attempt a check with a skill at rank 0 — you just lean entirely on the attribute.

Encumbrance Heavy armour applies a penalty to physical skills — stealth, athletics, dodge and melee. A riot shell keeps you alive and slows you down.

06Combat

Combat is round-based. Initiative (REF + a d10) is rolled once at the start and held for the fight. On your turn you take one action; then every living enemy acts; then end-of-round status effects tick.

Your actions

  • Attack — fire or strike with your equipped weapon. Ranged weapons fire up to their rate of fire (ROF) per round, spending loaded ammo. Hit location is rolled randomly (see table).
  • Aimed shot — a single, deliberate round to the head at a steep to-hit penalty (−6). If it lands, it's the head location's ×2 damage and a strong chance to stun. High risk, high reward — the classic Cyberpunk called shot.
  • Switch weapon — swap between the weapons you're carrying, ranged or melee, mid-fight. Shown when you carry more than one. The swap is free: it doesn't cost your action, so you can switch and then act the same round.
  • Reload — top up the magazine from your ammo reserve. Costs your action.
  • Use item — a consumable or tool mid-fight (healing needs the Medic passive).
  • Flee — an opposed athletics check against the fastest enemy. Fail, and everyone gets a free shot at you. Fleeing raises local heat.

Hit locations

A d10 decides where a hit lands and multiplies its damage. Head shots are lethal but rare on a random roll — which is what makes the aimed shot worth the penalty.

Hit location (d10)
RollLocationDamage ×Effect
1Head×2.0Chance to stun; jars cyberware
2–5Torso×1.0Full damage
6–7Arm×0.7Reduced; may disarm
8–10Leg×0.7Reduced; may cripple (REF penalty)

The damage pipeline

weapon dice (+ BOD bonus for melee) + (margin / 4)
  → hit-location multiplier
  → minus (target armour - weapon AP)
  → target HP

The margin term is why skill matters past hit-or-miss: a better shooter not only connects more often, they land harder when they do. Weapons in poor condition can jam and cost you the round; every attack wears the weapon down slightly.

07Damage, Armour & Wounds

Armour

Armour has Stopping Power (SP), subtracted from incoming damage before it reaches your HP. A weapon's Armour Penetration (AP) is subtracted from your SP first. Armour ablates: it loses condition as it soaks hits, and shot-up armour stops less. Some damage — fire, poison, hazards, bleeding — bypasses armour entirely.

Armour

Wound states

Being hurt makes everything harder. As your HP drops, a penalty applies to your own actions (attacks and skill checks). It does not cascade into your defence, so a bad wound is a real setback but never an automatic death spiral.

Wound states

Bleeding & heavy trauma

  • A hit of 8+ damage can open a bleeding wound: damage every turn until treated. A Trauma Kit stops it.
  • A hit of 10+ damage jars your cyberware, dropping its integrity.
  • Other status effects you'll meet: stunned burning poisoned suppressed stimmed → crash emp_locked dissociated drowning.
Two ways to die HP zero is the obvious one. Coherence zero is the other — and it is permanent and self-inflicted. See the next section before you buy your third implant.

08Cyberware & Coherence

Chrome is the most direct route to power in the Bay, and its most dangerous resource sink. A Coherence build and a chrome build are genuinely different playthroughs — and the ending you can reach depends partly on which you chose.

Coherence

Coherence starts at 100 and only ever goes down, permanently. Each implant inflicts its strain value as a permanent reduction — it's the integration between your nervous system and the hardware bolted into it. As Coherence falls you drift out of yourself.

Coherence bands
RangeBandEffect
90–100IntegratedNo penalty.
70–89Drifting−1 PRE.
50–69Fraying−1 PRE, −1 NRV; intrusive sensory noise.
30–49Dissociating−2 PRE, −2 NRV; forced composure checks under stress.
10–29Fugue−3 PRE, −3 NRV; possible hostile misidentification.
0–9FlatlineThe run ends.

Recovering Coherence is rare and expensive: downtime, therapy, or pulling chrome back out. Removing an implant returns only half its strain — the scar tissue is both literal and not. Back-alley installs are cheaper but cost extra Coherence and start the implant pre-degraded.

Integrity & misfire

Separate from Coherence, each implant has an integrity from 0–100. It drops when you take damage, use an implant's active ability, dive the net, or eat an EMP.

  • Below 50 integrity: the implant's attribute bonus is halved.
  • Below 25: it can misfire on use.
  • At 0: it goes offline until repaired (clinic, toolkit, or Techie passive).
Slots One implant per slot — head, eyes, spine, throat, arm, gland — except body, which takes three. A neural interface port is required before you can netrun at all.

09Netrunning

To dive you need a neural interface port installed and a deck equipped. A host is a stack of nodes you descend through, one at a time. At each node you choose how to get past what's guarding it.

The trace clock

Trace is a 0–100 meter that persists between runs. It does not reset when you jack out; it decays slowly over world turns. Every run raises a clock that follows you back into meatspace.

Trace bands
RangeBandWhat it means
0–24CleanNothing.
25–49FlaggedCRA encounter weight rises.
50–74TrackedHostile runners may counter-dive you.
75–94PinnedA strike team is being dispatched.
95–100BurnedThey have your address. They come.

Node actions

  • Probe — read what's at a node without engaging it. Small trace.
  • Crack — an intrusion check to take the node. Medium trace.
  • Stealth past — bypass the ICE entirely on a hard check. Low trace.
  • Attack ICE — fight the countermeasure with a loaded attack program.
  • Pull payload — extract a cracked node's contents (data, credits).
  • Descend — move to the next node deeper in the host.
  • Cauterise — emergency trace purge, one use per run (needs the program).
  • Jack out — leave, keeping everything you extracted so far.

Deck buffer & ICE

Your deck's buffer is your health in the net. ICE attacks deplete it. When the buffer hits zero, further ICE damage carries through the neural port into your real HP, and lethal (black) ICE degrades your cyberware on every hit. A cheap deck isn't just slower — it's the only thing between your brain and a Verdict.

Reputation & friction

Standing with each faction runs from −100 (blood feud) to +100 (made), through the tiers hostile wary neutral trusted inner. The catch is friction: gaining standing with one faction costs you standing with its rivals. Help the Lowline and Halcyon notices. You cannot max everyone — and where you end up gates which endings you can reach.

Asymmetry Friction cascades at full rate when you gain standing, and half rate when you lose it. The city is quicker to notice you helping an enemy than to forgive you hurting one.

Heat

Heat is per-district attention, 0–100. Killing people, fleeing fights, and drawing the CRA all raise it. High heat raises encounter frequency and the odds those encounters are a hard response. It decays on its own — fastest in districts you've left. Sometimes the move is simply to leave town for a few turns.

12Advancement & Endings

XP, levels, skill points

You earn XP from fights, discoveries and social wins. XP thresholds scale quadratically: reaching level n needs n² × 100 XP. Each level raises your max HP and grants 3 skill points, spent with the train command to raise skills (cap 10 each).

The story

The narrative is a graph of nodes, not a script. Every turn, the engine checks whether world state — where you went, who you angered, how much chrome you took, how hot you ran — has opened a new thread. Open available threads with the story command. The same world can reach different beats depending entirely on how you played it, and there are multiple reachable endings. Some are gated on Coherence: the "you're still yourself" endings need you intact.

Replayability Six roles, a friction-driven political map you can't fully satisfy, a Coherence-vs-chrome build axis, and a story pulled by world state rather than pushed by a fixed order. No two runs line up.

Verdigris Bay is an original work. The city, its six factions, every weapon, implant, program and the whole story arc are written for this project. It shares only the cyberpunk genre's common mechanical vocabulary — attribute + skill + die versus a difficulty number, hit locations, armour as damage reduction, augmentation with a psychological cost, a trace clock during intrusion — with the tabletop tradition it's built in the spirit of.
